# Meditation basics

Meditation is a family of practices for training attention and awareness,
usually to reach a mentally clear, calm and stable state. In the eight-limb
framework it corresponds to dharana (concentration) deepening into dhyana
(sustained meditation).

## Common approaches

- **Focused attention** -- sustaining attention on a single object: the
  breath, a candle flame, a mantra, or a point on the body. When the mind
  wanders (it will), attention is gently redirected back, without
  self-criticism. The redirection itself is the practice, not a failure of
  it.
- **Open monitoring / mindfulness** -- observing whatever arises (thoughts,
  sensations, sounds) without fixing on one object and without judgement,
  simply noticing and letting each thing pass.
- **Body scan** -- systematically moving attention through the body from
  head to toe or toe to head, noticing sensation in each area without
  trying to change it.
- **Loving-kindness (Metta)** -- silently directing goodwill and compassion
  toward oneself, then progressively toward others.

## Getting started

- A short, consistent daily practice (even 5-10 minutes) tends to build the
  habit more reliably than infrequent long sessions.
- Sitting upright but not rigid -- on a cushion, chair, or against a wall --
  supports alertness better than lying down, which more easily slides into
  sleep.
- Eyes may be closed or held in a soft, downward, unfocused gaze.
- Restlessness and a wandering mind are the normal texture of the practice,
  not signs it isn't working.

## Where it fits after asana practice

Many traditions place seated meditation after physical postures and
pranayama: the body is settled, and stillness is easier to sustain. A short
meditation to close a session -- even two or three minutes of quiet
breath-watching after the final relaxation pose (Savasana) -- is a common
and accessible way to include this limb without a separate, longer sitting
practice.
