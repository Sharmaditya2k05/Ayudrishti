# Yoga: origins and philosophy

Yoga is a physical, mental and spiritual practice that originated in ancient
India, with roots traceable to at least 2,000 years ago in texts such as the
Yoga Sutras of Patanjali. The word "yoga" comes from the Sanskrit root *yuj*,
meaning "to yoke" or "to unite" -- traditionally, the union of individual
consciousness with universal consciousness, and more practically, the
integration of body, breath and mind.

## The eight limbs (Ashtanga)

Patanjali's Yoga Sutras describe eight interconnected limbs of practice:

1. **Yama** -- ethical restraints (non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing,
   moderation, non-possessiveness).
2. **Niyama** -- personal observances (cleanliness, contentment, discipline,
   self-study, surrender).
3. **Asana** -- physical postures. This is the limb most associated with
   "yoga" in modern practice, but traditionally it is one of eight, not the
   whole of it.
4. **Pranayama** -- breath control and regulation.
5. **Pratyahara** -- withdrawal of the senses from external distraction.
6. **Dharana** -- concentration on a single point of focus.
7. **Dhyana** -- sustained meditation.
8. **Samadhi** -- a state of absorption or union.

## Styles in modern practice

- **Hatha** -- a broad umbrella term for physical yoga practices; often used
  today to describe a slower-paced, foundational class.
- **Vinyasa** -- poses linked fluidly to the breath, one movement per inhale
  or exhale.
- **Ashtanga** -- a fixed, vigorous sequence of postures performed in a set
  order.
- **Iyengar** -- precise alignment, often using props (blocks, straps,
  bolsters) to support correct form.
- **Restorative / Yin** -- long-held, passive poses that target connective
  tissue and the nervous system rather than muscular strength.
