# Getting started as a beginner

## What you actually need

Just enough space to stretch out your arms and legs in every direction, and
a mat if you have one (a folded blanket or rug works too). No special
equipment is required to begin -- AyuDrishti's camera-based assessment works
with nothing more than a phone or laptop camera positioned 2-3 metres away.

## How often to practice

Consistency matters more than duration. Three sessions a week of 15-20
minutes tends to build a habit and show visible improvement faster than one
long weekly session. AyuDrishti's recommendation engine asks for a target
practice frequency during onboarding and factors it into session planning
and progress commentary.

## What a beginner session usually looks like

A well-formed session moves through a simple arc: a brief warm-up, a small
set of standing or seated postures matched to current ability, and a
cooldown pose to close (commonly a forward fold, Child's Pose, or a few
breaths of stillness). Poses are usually held for 20-50 seconds depending on
the posture and the practitioner's level -- long enough to settle into and
be assessed properly, short enough to stay safe and repeatable.

## Common beginner concerns

- **"I'm not flexible enough for yoga."** Flexibility is a result of
  practice, not a prerequisite for starting it. Every pose has a beginner
  entry point.
- **Balance poses are wobbly at first.** This is completely normal and
  improves with repetition -- the wobble is the nervous system calibrating,
  not a sign of doing it wrong.
- **Soreness after the first few sessions.** Mild muscle soreness a day
  after new movement is ordinary. Sharp pain during a pose is not, and is a
  signal to ease off (see the safety notes for more on this distinction).

## How AyuDrishti personalises from here

Your stated goals, experience level, and any focus areas or poses to avoid
(set in your profile) directly shape which poses are recommended and how
they're scored against your level -- and once you have a few completed
sessions, your actual measured performance (which poses you're mastering,
which need more work) starts adjusting future sessions automatically.
