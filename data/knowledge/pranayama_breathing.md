# Pranayama: breath control

Pranayama (*prana* = life force / breath, *ayama* = extension or control) is
the practice of consciously regulating the breath. It is traditionally
practised seated, after asana and before meditation, though many teachers
introduce simple breath awareness at any point in a session.

## Common techniques

- **Ujjayi (victorious breath)** -- a slight constriction at the back of the
  throat creates an audible, ocean-like sound on both inhale and exhale.
  Commonly used throughout Vinyasa and Ashtanga practice to anchor movement
  to breath.
- **Nadi Shodhana (alternate nostril breathing)** -- breathing is alternated
  between the left and right nostril using the fingers to close one side at
  a time. Practised for calming and balancing the nervous system, typically
  seated and still.
- **Kapalabhati (skull-shining breath)** -- short, forceful exhales through
  the nose with passive inhales, performed in rapid succession. An
  energising, warming technique -- not recommended during pregnancy or with
  high blood pressure, and usually taught only once a student is comfortable
  with basic breath control.
- **Dirga (three-part breath)** -- a slow, full breath consciously expanded
  through the belly, ribcage and chest in sequence, then released in the
  same order. A gentle, foundational technique often used to introduce
  breath awareness to beginners.
- **Sitali / Sitkari (cooling breath)** -- inhaling through a curled tongue
  or clenched teeth to cool incoming air. Used for its calming, cooling
  effect, particularly in warmer conditions or after vigorous practice.

## Why it matters in an assessment context

Breath quality is a genuine signal of how a pose is being held: laboured,
held, or irregular breathing during an asana often indicates the pose is
being forced beyond a comfortable range, or that the practitioner is
bracing rather than engaging appropriately. Steady, even breath throughout
a hold is one of the simplest self-checks a practitioner can use, alongside
any posture feedback.
